# Offline evaluation results — 2026-09-21

> **Corrected 2026-09-21, after review.** The first version of this document claimed that
> "forecasting works" on the strength of an operational replay, and reported level-shift,
> spike and weekly scenarios as if they had been tested. Both claims were wrong. The
> operational numbers are **withdrawn** (the replay is a flawed approximation of the
> controller — see *Withdrawn*), and the stress scenarios **never entered the scored
> window**, so those rows measured ordinary repeating traffic. The recommendation to remove
> the network is also withdrawn: it rested on evidence this run does not provide.
> What survives is narrower, and is stated below.

## What this run actually establishes

**Large and unstable neural forecast errors on synthetic, repeating daily traffic.**

That is the whole claim. It is worth making, because the instability is severe and
reproducible, but it is not a verdict on predictive autoscaling, on the network's value in
general, or on behaviour during regime change.

### The instability, on identical inputs

The strongest evidence does not depend on the scenario labels at all. Three runs were
generated from **the same series** in the scored window (the "level shift" event lay outside
it, so `repeating` and `levelshift` are byte-identical there; `spike` differs only in the
noise stream). The baselines confirm the data identity — they return the same numbers to the
decimal. The network does not:

| Data | network MAE, run A | run B | run C | previous-day MAE (all runs) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| seed 1 | 417.8 | 433.4 | 1894.3 | 142.4 / 142.4 / 142.7 |
| seed 2 | 6055.1 | **3.4×10¹³** | 489.8 | 135.9 / 135.9 / 136.7 |

Same data, same code, same hyper-parameters. The only difference is the random
initialisation, which is **not seeded** (a defect in the harness, now recorded). Run-to-run
spread on identical input spans **ten orders of magnitude**. Whatever else is true, a
forecaster whose error varies by 10¹⁰ between runs on the same data is not ready to be
relied on.

### Accuracy on the runs that are interpretable

Only the `repeating` (3 seeds) and `trend` (2 seeds) runs are interpretable, and only as
"steady daily profile, mild trend". MAE, lower is better:

| Scenario | seed | served blend | network only | seasonal pattern | previous day | persistence | trend adaptive |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| repeating | 1 | 147.9 | 417.8 | **131.1** | 142.4 | 270.4 | 148.1 |
| repeating | 2 | 1021.1 | 6055.1 | **129.9** | 135.9 | 266.2 | 142.6 |
| repeating | 3 | 152.2 | 463.4 | **125.1** | 128.4 | 268.3 | 135.3 |
| trend | 1 | 278.4 | 794.7 | 210.1 | 220.3 | 317.4 | **176.4** |
| trend | 2 | 220.9 | 499.5 | 204.2 | 216.9 | 312.0 | **170.1** |

On these five runs the network is the worst forecaster tested and the preregistered bar —
10% lower MAE than the strongest baseline — is not approached. The strongest baseline is the
seasonal pattern on repeating traffic and the trend-adaptive rule when the level moves.

This does **not** license "remove the network". Five runs on one synthetic profile, with an
unseeded initialisation and a training configuration that is not production-equivalent
(12 epochs against the trainer's 50, expanding history rather than the rolling seven-day
workflow), cannot settle that question. It licenses one bounded stabilisation attempt.

## Withdrawn

- **All operational-benefit numbers.** The replay is a Python approximation of the Go
  controller, missing the ramp safeguards, sanity rejection, confidence handling and cache
  behaviour. It also has three defects of its own: pending readiness updates are overwritten
  before they take effect; two-minute readiness is judged on ten-minute ticks, so every
  deficit is counted as a full ten minutes; and demand is clipped to the replica ceiling,
  which hides overload rather than reporting it. Nothing about "forecast versus reactive"
  follows from it. A rebuilt replay with event-time capacity accounting and differential
  tests against the real Go controller is required before any such claim is made again.
- **"Forecasting works."** That conclusion rested entirely on the withdrawn replay.
- **"Remove the network."** Not supported by five runs on one profile.
- **Level-shift, spike and weekly results.** Origins covered series indices 288–687 with the
  last target at 693. The level shift is introduced at index 1071 and the earliest spike at
  950; the weekly scenario's scored window (2026-03-03 to 03-05) contains no weekend. None
  of the three events was ever inside the evaluation window. Those rows measured ordinary
  repeating traffic and have been removed from the accuracy table above.
- **"All failure modes handled."** The checks that ran are listed below; two of the eight
  did not test what their names claimed.
- **The "+10min worse than +60min" reading.** For the raw network the numbers run the other
  way (426.8 at +10 against 495.7 at +60). The reversal belongs to the blend, whose weights
  change with the horizon, and neither ordering diagnoses a cause.

## Failure-mode checks — what ran, and what did not

| Condition | Result |
|---|---|
| Cold start, less than one window | Refused cleanly: "Insufficient data: 72 points (need 150)" |
| All-zero series | Finite output, no crash |
| Gap in the seasonal history | Pattern sourced from real history; no invented values |
| Delayed observation (40 min stale) | Forecast anchored to the data's clock, not the wall clock |
| Old-format artifact | Detected and refused |
| Retrain landing mid-peak | Completed without error |
| **Level-shift recovery** | **NOT SCORED** — the event fell outside the evaluation window |
| **Spike recovery** | **NOT SCORED** — same defect |

The first six are genuine. The last two are not results and must not be quoted as such.

## Known defects in this harness, to be fixed before the next run

1. Evaluation windows are not asserted to contain their scenario's event (cause of the two
   unscored rows above).
2. The operational replay is withdrawn pending a rebuild against the real controller.
3. TensorFlow initialisation is unseeded; data and model seeds are not recorded separately.
4. Training is not production-equivalent: 12 epochs against 50, expanding history rather
   than the rolling seven-day/outer-split workflow, capacity calibrated on the whole future
   series rather than on training data only.
5. Network scores cover 398 origins against the baselines' 400 — origins must be paired, and
   failures preserved rather than skipped.
6. The synthetic profile is a smooth cosine; the real generator uses an hourly step profile.
7. The train/validation purge in `ml-engine/models/lstm_model.py` demands a larger gap than
   necessary and falls back to the contaminated overlapping split when the series is short —
   which is what happened in **every one of these runs**, so the leakage fix was inert here.

## Reproducing

```sh
eval/.venv/bin/python eval/offline_eval.py --full --out eval/results.json
eval/.venv/bin/python eval/offline_eval.py --failure-modes
```

Raw output: `eval/results-20260921.json`, `eval/failure-modes.json`. Synthetic series come
from `make_series()` with the stated seeds. `eval/data/benchmark-nginx-test.json` is a
read-only Prometheus export from the benchmark cluster; it was **not** scored — 248
ten-minute points is below the 288 needed for the warm-up plus rolling origins.

## What comes next

1. Fix the harness defects listed above, starting with the window assertions.
2. Rebuild the operational replay with event-time capacity accounting and differential tests
   against the actual Go controller.
3. Make the training configuration production-equivalent and seed it.
4. One bounded stabilisation experiment: `tanh` activation as one arm, gradient clipping as a
   separate arm, never both at once, judged against the strongest baseline rather than
   previous-day alone. Invalid forecasts to be rejected with a documented fallback, with raw
   and guarded outputs reported separately.
